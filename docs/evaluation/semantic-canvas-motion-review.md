# Semantic Canvas motion review (synthetic)

This review uses synthetic Japanese meeting content and the actual Shared View browser renderer. It tests presentation transitions, not STT or Analyzer quality. No real meeting recording, transcript, or provider output is included.

The 21 scenes cover: empty Canvas; issue and response; supports and opposes; sibling options; Open Item; an independently confirmed discussion entry; branch return; late relation; relation correction without relocation; Candidate Decision and explicit Human confirmation; Action; long Canonical subtitle; 15, 30, 60, and 100 Nodes; and meeting-end zoom-out. Node and subtitle timestamps are synthetic graph-update times, shown in Japan time. The expanded 15–100 Node cases are load/semantic-zoom fixtures, not realistic generated meeting semantics.

Regenerate locally from the repository root:

```sh
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python tests/canvas_motion_fixture.py > tmp/canvas-motion-scenes.json
.venv/bin/python -m prototype.server --host 127.0.0.1 --port 18080 --live-ws-port 18765
# In another terminal, with Playwright available in NODE_PATH:
node tests/canvas_motion_video.cjs
```

The generated 1920×1080 MP4 is `tmp/ronro-semantic-canvas-motion.mp4`; it is intentionally ignored by Git. The frame images are under `tmp/canvas-motion/`, including a lossless `final-review.png` for inspecting relation captions without JPEG compression. The browser renderer checks focus, the five-Node Live limit, timestamps, scrolling, and Final markers. Unit tests check event progression, stable placement through return/correction, and explicit Candidate-to-Confirmed transition.

To reduce short orphaned wraps without shrinking text, Live Node width is 390 px (previously 360) and Final representative width is 290 px (previously 240). Display-only Japanese word segmentation keeps short terms such as 「通行止め」 together, while `auto-phrase`/`pretty` wrapping chooses natural line breaks where the browser supports it. Unusually long individual tokens remain breakable as a safety fallback. The Final callout collision spacing is increased accordingly. Browser QA checks primary/card overlap, clipping, and label overflow; the motion fixture checks that 「通行止め」 is intact and short Option/Decision labels remain one line at 1920×1080. Canonical text and font sizes are unchanged.

The faded peripheral layer sits behind primary Nodes, so it may share screen space without obscuring primary text. Semantic lines have no text labels; compact arrowheads mark their direction without covering Node text. Blue open arrows show discussion development, green filled arrows show support, and warm double-chevron arrows show opposition. When the discussion backbone and an argument enter the same side of a Node, the backbone lands at the nearby corner while the argument keeps a side port; slight spacing alone still looked like a doubled tip. These arrows describe discussion relations, not physical causality. At meeting end, independently confirmed discussion entries have a subtle solid outline, while nodes whose discussion-origin relation is still unconfirmed retain a dashed outline. Neither receives a participant-facing root-status caption: the Node's own label communicates its subject. Representative branches and Decision/Open Item/Action callouts remain on the same Canvas. To avoid overlap, callouts may move slightly; a bounded final-view adjustment also tries nearby positions when lines cross or pass through another card. It does not move the underlying Live Canvas positions or invent Relations, and unavoidable crossings remain possible. Final semantic lines terminate just outside the displayed callout borders rather than at invisible original node boxes. Only relations whose two endpoints are shown receive a line. Final lines are continuous. The previous faint dot-to-callout leaders and detached zoomed-out world edges are removed. The video contains no test-only text overlay. The local browser QA also checks fixed Node width/type size during focus changes and counts for currently active Nodes by participant-facing category. The video does not establish physical 3–5 m readability or real Analyzer behavior.
